# Crop Rotation Plan

**Goal:** Avoid planting the same crop family in the same bed in consecutive years. This reduces soil-borne disease, manages nutrient depletion, and breaks pest cycles.

---

## Crop Families in This Garden

| Family | Crops | 2026 Bed |
|---|---|---|
| Solanaceae | Potatoes | Root Bed A |
| Apiaceae | Carrots, parsnips | Root Bed B |
| Brassicaceae | Cabbage, kale, broccoli, kohlrabi, turnips, rutabaga | Brassica Bed |
| Amaryllidaceae | Shallots, garlic | Allium Bed |
| Amaranthaceae | Beets, spinach | Brassica Bed (trial strips) |

> **Note:** Turnips and rutabaga are Brassicaceae — they rotate with the brassicas, not with the beets (Amaranthaceae). Track them separately.

## Rotation Rules of Thumb

1. **Potatoes** should not return to the same bed for at least 2 years (blight and scab risk).
2. **Brassicas** should not follow brassicas — clubroot can persist in soil for 5+ years in severe cases.
3. **Carrots** benefit from following alliums (residual scent may reduce carrot fly).
4. **Alliums** are light feeders and good predecessors for heavy feeders like brassicas.
5. **Legumes** (planned for year two) fix nitrogen — place them *before* brassicas or potatoes to provide a nutrient boost.

## Long-Term Notes

- Because beds are fixed sizes, rotation may need to be partial or split (e.g., half of Brassica Bed gets potatoes, half keeps kale).
- As the property moves toward agroforestry, perennial beds may absorb some annual rotation pressure.
