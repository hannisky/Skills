## April — Soil Preparation

**Only begin once the squeeze test passes:** take a handful of soil and squeeze — it must crumble when released, not form a sticky ball. Never work waterlogged clay.

### 🏗️ Fencing & Structure (do first — can be done while soil is still wet)
- [ ] Build or repair **deer/hare perimeter fencing** around the entire garden.
- [ ] Install **net support hoops** over Root Bed A, Root Bed B, and Brassica Bed (hoops go in before netting is needed in May).
- [ ] Clear and level the **Trellis Raised Beds** (TRB 1–3); check lids and latches; refresh growing medium.

### 🪴 Bed Preparation (when soil is workable)
- [ ] **Lightly fork** all open beds to 20–25 cm — do **not** invert or deep-turn clay.
- [ ] **Deep-fork Root Bed B** to ~30 cm and work in a thin layer of sand and compost to improve tilth for carrots and parsnips.
- [ ] **Topdress all beds** with 3–5 cm of compost/well-rotted manure.
- [ ] **Mulch all paths** with 5–10 cm of hay to suppress weeds and protect soil structure.

## Quick Reference: Why These Steps Matter

| Action | Reason |
|---|---|
| Order seeds now | Supply shortages are common; early-variety stock sells out |
| Don't touch wet clay | Working wet clay destroys soil structure for the whole season |
| Build fencing before planting | Deer and hare damage is immediate once green growth appears |
| Install hoops before netting | Easier to fit hoops into empty beds; netting goes on at sowing/planting time |
| Topdress with compost | Annual organic matter addition is the core strategy for improving heavy clay |
