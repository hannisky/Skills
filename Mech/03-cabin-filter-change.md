# Cabin Air Filter (Pollen Filter) Replacement — Subaru Outback 2009 BP

**Service interval:** Every 20,000–30,000 km or annually (every 12–15,000 km in cities/high-pollen areas)  
**Difficulty:** ★☆☆☆☆ — Beginner  
**Time:** 10–20 minutes  
**Part:** Cabin Filter — applies to both petrol and diesel BP variants

> **"Leg/Diesel" in parts list:** This notation likely refers to the filter being compatible across the Outback BP, Legacy BL/BP, and diesel variants. All share the same cabin filter housing and footprint.

---

## Why It Matters

The cabin (pollen) filter cleans air entering the vehicle's HVAC system (heating, ventilation, air conditioning). A clogged cabin filter causes:
- Reduced airflow from vents
- Musty or mouldy smell inside the cabin
- Fogged windows taking longer to clear
- Increased pollen and dust inside the car

## Location

The cabin filter on the BP Outback is located **behind the glove box**, inside the HVAC ducting. It is accessed by opening or partially dropping the glove box.

---

## Step-by-Step

### 1. Open the Glove Box
- Open the glove box fully.

### 2. Squeeze and Drop the Glove Box
- On either side of the open glove box, there are **stop tabs/bumpers** that prevent it from opening past a certain point.
- **Squeeze the sides of the glove box inward** (compress both sides simultaneously) to release it from its stop tabs.
- Allow the glove box to swing **fully down** — it will hang open, exposing the area behind it.

### 3. Locate the Filter Cover
- You will see a rectangular plastic cover/door on the HVAC housing behind the glove box.
- It is held by a **tab or small clip** on one side.

### 4. Remove the Cover
- Slide or press the tab/clip to release the cover.
- Pull the cover away and set aside.

### 5. Slide Out the Old Filter
- The filter slides straight out toward you.
- Note the **airflow direction arrow** on the old filter — the new one must go in the same way.
- The arrow typically points **downward** or toward the blower (check before discarding old filter).

### 6. Check and Clean the Housing
- Look inside the housing for any debris, leaves, or insects.
- Wipe the housing with a dry cloth if dirty.
- If the housing smells musty, a quick spray of HVAC sanitiser / evaporator cleaner into the duct at this point is beneficial.

### 7. Install New Filter
- Slide the new filter in with the **arrow in the correct direction** (same as old filter).
- Push fully until it seats flush.

### 8. Refit the Cover
- Clip the cover back on.

### 9. Restore the Glove Box
- Squeeze the sides of the glove box again and swing it back up until the stop tabs re-engage.
- Close and test — it should open and close normally.

### 10. Test the HVAC
- Start the car, turn on the fan — airflow should be noticeably stronger if the old filter was clogged.

## Tips & Cross-Reference Notes

- **Legacy BL (2003–2009)**: essentially identical procedure — same glove box squeeze method, same filter housing location.
- **Forester SH (2008–2012)**: same glove box drop method, slightly different housing clip — filter itself is different size.
- **Impreza GH/GR (2007–2011)**: same design, same procedure.
- Some BP models have a **two-layer filter** (particulate + activated carbon for odours). If you live near traffic or want better odour filtration, upgrade to a carbon-layer filter.
- Check the filter every year even if not replacing — in low-pollen, clean-air areas it may last longer.

## Disposal

Standard household waste — not hazardous (unless excessively contaminated with mould/chemicals).
