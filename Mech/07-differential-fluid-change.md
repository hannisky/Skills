# Differential Fluid Change — Subaru Outback 2009 BP (AWD)

**Service interval:** Every 40,000–60,000 km or every 4 years  
**Difficulty:** ★★☆☆☆ — Beginner/Intermediate  
**Time:** 45–75 minutes (rear diff) / 30 minutes (front diff)  
**Fluid:** Hypoid Gear Oil GL-5 80W-90  
**Applies to:** Rear differential (and front if applicable — see note below)

---

## Why It Matters

The Subaru Outback BP uses a **symmetrical AWD system**. Differential fluid lubricates:
- Hypoid ring-and-pinion gears (which require GL-5 hypoid-rated oil — the curved tooth geometry creates extreme pressure not handled by GL-4)
- Planet gears and spider gears in open or limited-slip differentials
- Bearings throughout the differential

Old fluid causes:
- Excessive differential wear
- Howling or whining noise from rear
- Overheating under towing or off-road use
- Premature bearing failure

---

## Understanding the BP Outback AWD Differential Setup

| Component | Location | Fluid |
|-----------|----------|-------|
| Rear differential | Rear axle | GL-5 80W-90 hypoid gear oil |
| Front differential (integrated with gearbox) | Usually shares gearbox fill | **GL-4** gear oil — same as gearbox |
| Centre viscous coupling / transfer | Internal to gearbox/transfer | Typically gearbox fluid — **not a separate service** |

> **Key point:** The **GL-5 80W-90** listed in your parts is for the **rear differential**. The front differential on manual BP Outbacks is typically integrated with the gearbox and shares gearbox oil — **do not use GL-5 there** (see guide 06).

> On **automatic transmission** BP Outbacks: the front differential is in the automatic transmission and uses ATF. Only the rear diff uses GL-5 gear oil as a separate service.

---

## Fluid Specification

- **GL-5 80W-90 hypoid gear oil** — suitable for the rear differential's hypoid gear set.
- This is the correct spec because hypoid gears (where the pinion centre is below the ring gear centre) generate extreme sliding contact pressures, requiring the EP (extreme pressure) additives in GL-5.
- Do **not** use GL-5 in the manual gearbox (only the rear differential).

## Step-by-Step — Rear Differential

### 1. Lift and Support
- Jack up the rear of the vehicle (or full vehicle) and place on jack stands.
- Ensure the car is **level** — critical for correct fill level.

### 2. Locate the Plugs
- The rear differential is the **round/oval casting mounted in the centre of the rear axle**.
- Identify the **fill plug** (side of the diff, mid-height) and **drain plug** (bottom of the diff).
- Clean around both plugs.

### 3. Open the Fill Plug First
- 24mm socket, loosen counter-clockwise.
- Confirm it moves — do not drain without being able to refill.

### 4. Drain Old Fluid
- Place drain pan under diff.
- Remove drain plug and drain completely (5–10 minutes).
- Inspect old fluid — a slightly copper/reddish tinge is normal from gear wear dust. Excessive metal particles or a very dark/black fluid suggests the diff has been running dry or is worn.

### 5. Refit Drain Plug
- Clean plug, new crush washer, torque to **44 Nm**.

### 6. Refill with GL-5 80W-90
- Pump oil in through the fill hole until it weeps from the hole.
- Leave 1–2 minutes before plugging, allowing air to escape and fluid level to settle.
- Approximately **1.0–1.2 litres** for the rear differential.

### 7. Refit Fill Plug
- New crush washer, torque to **44 Nm**.

### 8. Lower and Check
- Lower the vehicle.
- Check both plugs for leaks after driving.

## Step-by-Step — Front Differential (Auto Transmission Variant)

On automatic BP Outbacks, the front differential is inside the automatic transmission housing and **uses ATF, not gear oil**. It is serviced as part of the transmission fluid change (not covered in this guide). No separate drain plug exists.

On **manual BP Outbacks**, the front differential shares the gearbox case — see **guide 06** for that procedure. Do not use GL-5 in that location.

---

## Limited Slip Differential (LSD) — If Fitted

Some BP Outback variants came with a **Torsen or viscous limited-slip rear differential**. If yours has an LSD:
- Use an **LSD-compatible GL-5 80W-90** or add an LSD friction modifier additive.
- Incorrect fluid in an LSD causes chatter and shudder on tight turns.
- Check your VIN / build sheet to confirm if LSD is fitted.

---

## Tips & Cross-Reference Notes

- **Forester SH (2008–2012)**: same rear differential design — same fluid, same procedure, same torque.
- **Legacy BL/BP (2003–2009)**: identical rear diff setup.
- **Signs you need to do this sooner**: whining from the rear end under load, vibration during tight low-speed turns (LSD chatter).
- A front and rear diff change + gearbox change in one session is efficient — you're already under the car with the tools out.
- **Old fluid colour guide**: Honey/amber = fresh. Brown = used but OK. Black/metallic = overdue or problem.

---

## Disposal

Used gear oil is hazardous waste. Store sealed, take to a recycling centre or auto parts store oil bank.
