# Oil Drain Plug Gasket — Subaru Outback 2009 BP

**Used during:** Engine oil change, transmission fluid drain, differential fluid drain  
**Difficulty:** ★☆☆☆☆ — Part of any drain/fill service  
**Part:** Gasket / Crush Washer — 26.3 × 32.3 × 1 mm  

---

## What Is This Part?

This is an **aluminium crush washer (sealing gasket)** used on drain plugs across multiple Subaru fluid systems. When the drain plug bolt is torqued down, the soft aluminium washer deforms slightly to create a leak-proof seal between the plug and the drain hole.

**Size 26.3 × 32.3 × 1 mm** corresponds to a common Subaru drain plug washer used on:
- Engine oil sump drain plug
- Manual gearbox drain/fill plug
- Differential drain/fill plug

> **Critical rule:** Always replace the crush washer every time a drain plug is removed. Reusing the old deformed washer risks leaks.

---

## Why It Matters

- A missing or reused crush washer = **oil leak**
- Over-torquing to compensate for no washer = **stripped or cracked sump threads** (expensive repair)
- Under-torquing = **drain plug falls out** (catastrophic oil loss while driving)

## Step-by-Step (as Part of Oil Change)

### 1. Warm the Engine (Optional but recommended)
- Running the engine for 5 minutes before draining helps fluid flow more freely and carries suspended contaminants out with the old oil.
- Do not drain immediately after full running temp — hot oil burns.

### 2. Lift and Support
- Jack the car to a safe working height and support on jack stands.
- Alternatively, use ramps.

### 3. Prepare
- Place drain pan under the sump/diff drain plug.
- Have the new crush washer and correct socket ready.

### 4. Remove the Drain Plug
- Use the correct socket to loosen the drain plug **counter-clockwise**.
- Once loose, use your fingers to spin it the rest of the way — keep control so you don't drop it into the pan.
- Let the fluid drain completely (5–10 minutes).

### 5. Remove the Old Washer
- The old crush washer may fall into the drain pan — retrieve and discard it.
- If it stays on the plug, slide it off.
- **Never reuse it.**

### 6. Clean the Drain Plug
- Wipe the plug and threads clean with a rag.
- Inspect the plug threads for damage. If threads look damaged, replace the plug.

### 7. Fit New Crush Washer
- Slide the new 26.3 × 32.3 × 1mm washer onto the drain plug.

### 8. Reinstall the Drain Plug
- Thread in by hand first (prevents cross-threading).
- Torque to spec with a torque wrench — **44 Nm** in most cases.

### 9. Top Up Fluid and Check
- Refill with the appropriate fluid (engine oil, gearbox oil, etc.).
- Run briefly, then check underneath for leaks.

## Buying Tips

- Buy a **10-pack** — they're cheap (< $1 each) and you'll use them across every drain service.
- The same washer size is used on multiple Subaru models of this era (Forester, Impreza, Legacy) — a bulk pack covers all of them.
- Avoid **rubber-coated** or fibre washers as replacements — use aluminium crush type.
