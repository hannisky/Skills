# Brake Fluid Flush — Subaru Outback 2009 BP

**Service interval:** Every 2 years regardless of mileage (DOT4 absorbs moisture over time)  
**Difficulty:** ★★☆☆☆ — Beginner/Intermediate  
**Time:** 45–90 minutes  
**Fluid:** Brake fluid DOT4 (Bromsvätska DOT4)  
**Capacity:** Approximately 0.5–1.0 litre for a full system flush

---

## Why It Matters

DOT4 brake fluid is **hygroscopic** — it absorbs water from the atmosphere over time. As moisture content rises:
- The boiling point drops significantly
- Risk of **brake fade** under hard or repeated braking increases (vapour bubbles in lines = soft or no pedal)
- Internal corrosion of brake callipers, wheel cylinders, and ABS components accelerates

A full flush every 2 years is mandatory maintenance, especially in Scandinavian/northern European climates with high humidity and road salt.

## Parts & Tools

| Item | Details |
|------|---------|
| DOT4 brake fluid | Minimum 1 litre — use **DOT4 only** (do not mix with DOT3; never use DOT5 silicone on this car) |
| Bleeding kit | Vacuum bleeder (easiest solo), or pressure bleeder, or assistant for pedal method |
| Clear plastic tubing | 6mm inner diameter to fit over bleed nipples |
| Catch bottle | Small jar or dedicated bleeder bottle |
| 7mm or 8mm spanner | For brake bleed nipples (verify — BP uses 8mm nipples typically) |
| Turkey baster or syringe | For extracting old fluid from reservoir |
| Clean rags | Brake fluid strips paint — cover bodywork near reservoir |

> **Warning:** Brake fluid is highly corrosive to paint. Work carefully — wipe any spills immediately.

---

## Bleeding Order (Subaru BP Outback)

Always bleed from **furthest from master cylinder first** to nearest:

1. Rear Right (Passenger Rear)
2. Rear Left (Driver Rear)
3. Front Right (Passenger Front)
4. Front Left (Driver Front — closest to master cylinder)

---

## Step-by-Step

### 1. Prepare the Reservoir
- Open the bonnet. The brake fluid reservoir is on the **driver's side of the engine bay**, at the base of the windscreen, connected to the master cylinder.
- Remove the cap.
- Use a turkey baster/syringe to **extract the old fluid** from the reservoir — dispose of it properly.
- Refill the reservoir with **fresh DOT4** to the MAX line.
- Keep the reservoir topped up throughout the entire process — **never let it run empty** (introduces air into lines).

### 2. Lift and Support the Vehicle
- Jack the car and place on jack stands, or use a 2-post lift.
- Remove all 4 wheels for better access (or bleed one corner at a time with just that wheel removed).

### 3. Bleed Each Corner — Starting Rear Right

**Per corner:**

a. Locate the bleed nipple on the back of the calliper or wheel cylinder.  
b. Remove the rubber dust cap from the nipple.  
c. Push one end of clear plastic tubing over the nipple; place the other end in your catch bottle.  
d. **Using a vacuum bleeder:** Attach the vacuum pump to the tubing. Open the nipple with the 8mm spanner (**counter-clockwise, ~¾ turn**). Pump until fluid runs clear and bubble-free (typically 50–100ml per corner).  
e. **Using the pedal method (with assistant):** Have assistant pump brakes 3–4 times, hold on last pump. Open nipple — fluid and bubbles come out. Close nipple. Repeat until clear.  
f. Close the nipple firmly (**do not overtighten** — nipples strip easily, ~8–10 Nm).  
g. Replace dust cap.  
h. **Top up the reservoir before moving to the next corner.**

### 4. Repeat for All 4 Corners (in order)
- Rear Right → Rear Left → Front Right → Front Left

### 5. Final Check
- Fill reservoir to MAX line with fresh DOT4.
- Refit reservoir cap.
- Check pedal feel — it should be firm and consistent, not spongy.
- A spongy pedal after bleeding = air still in system. Repeat bleeding.

### 6. Refit Wheels and Test
- Reinstall wheels, torque to spec (**120 Nm** for BP Outback wheel bolts).
- Lower the car.
- With the car stationary, pump the brake pedal firmly several times and verify it feels solid.
- Drive slowly in a safe area and test brakes before normal driving.

---

## ABS Bleed Note

If the **ABS pump** contains old fluid or air (rare unless ABS module was opened), it requires special cycling of the ABS pump using a diagnostic tool. For a standard fluid flush, normal bleeding is sufficient. If you have a spongy pedal that won't resolve, ABS pump bleeding may be required.

---

## Tips & Cross-Reference Notes

- **DOT4 fluid spec** is the same across the entire Subaru range of this era — Legacy BL/BP, Forester SH, Impreza GH — all use DOT4.
- In cold climates (Sweden, Norway, Finland), brake fluid absorbs moisture faster — consider doing this every 18 months or having fluid tested with a DOT4 moisture tester strip.
- **Never reuse old drained brake fluid.**
- Store unused new fluid in a sealed bottle — it starts absorbing moisture the moment it's opened.

---

## Disposal

Brake fluid is hazardous waste. Take to an auto shop, recycling centre, or council waste facility — **do not pour down drain**.
