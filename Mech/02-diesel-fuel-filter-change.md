# Diesel Fuel Filter Replacement — Subaru Outback 2009 BP (EE20 Diesel)

**Service interval:** Every 40,000–60,000 km or every 2 years  
**Difficulty:** ★★☆☆☆ — Beginner/Intermediate  
**Time:** 20–40 minutes  
**Part:** Cartridge Fuel Filter — specific to EE20 diesel engine variant

---

## Why It Matters

The diesel fuel filter removes water and particulates from fuel before it reaches the high-pressure common rail injection system. A blocked or water-saturated filter causes:
- Hard starting, especially when cold
- Loss of power under load
- Rough idle or misfiring
- In severe cases, injector damage (very expensive)

> **This guide applies to the diesel EE20 engine only.** The petrol EJ25/EJ20 Outback BP uses an *in-tank* sock filter integrated with the fuel pump — not a serviceable cartridge.

## Location

On the EE20 diesel Outback BP, the fuel filter housing is located in the **engine bay**, typically on the **driver's side** near the firewall or intake area. It is a cylindrical housing with a removable cap — look for the housing with fuel lines connected to it.

> If this is your first time, trace the fuel line from the fuel rail backwards toward the firewall — the filter housing will be inline before the high-pressure pump.

---

## Safety First

- **Do not work near open flames or sparks** — diesel is flammable.
- Have a **rag** ready for fuel spillage.
- Relieve fuel pressure before opening: run the engine until it stalls on residual fuel *or* remove the fuel pump fuse/relay and crank briefly.

---

## Step-by-Step

### 1. Prepare
- Allow engine to cool completely.
- Disconnect the **negative battery terminal** — prevents accidental cranking.
- Place rags under the filter housing.

### 2. Remove the Housing Cap
- Use the filter housing wrench to **turn the cap counter-clockwise**.
- Expect a small amount of diesel to drain — have rags ready.
- Fully remove the cap.

### 3. Extract the Old Cartridge
- Pull the old filter cartridge out of the housing.
- Dispose of it properly — diesel-soaked filters are hazardous waste.
- Note any water or unusual debris in the housing (may indicate larger fuel system issues).

### 4. Drain and Clean the Housing
- Tip the housing to drain any remaining diesel.
- Wipe the inside with a clean lint-free cloth.
- Inspect the O-ring groove on the cap.

### 5. Fit New O-Ring
- Remove the old O-ring from the cap.
- Lightly lubricate the new O-ring with clean diesel fuel.
- Seat it in the groove.

### 6. Install New Cartridge
- Insert the new filter cartridge with the correct orientation (arrow or TOP marking pointing up/toward the cap).
- Press it fully into the housing until seated.

### 7. Refit the Cap
- Thread the cap back on by hand first, then tighten with the wrench.
- **Torque to approximately 25 Nm** — do not overtighten (plastic housing threads).

### 8. Reconnect Battery and Prime
- Reconnect the negative battery terminal.
- Cycle the ignition key to ON (do not crank) **3–4 times**, holding it for 5 seconds each time.
- This primes the fuel system without cranking.
- Check for any fuel leaks around the cap.

### 9. Start and Check
- Start the engine — it may take slightly longer to start than usual the first time.
- Let it idle for 2–3 minutes.
- Inspect around the filter housing for leaks.

---

## Water Separator Drain (if equipped)

Some EE20 filter housings include a **water-in-fuel sensor** or drain screw at the bottom. If the water-in-fuel warning light has been on:
- Place a container under the drain point.
- Open the drain screw slightly until only clean fuel runs out.
- Retighten and clear the warning (may reset automatically after driving).

---

## Tips & Cross-Reference Notes

- **Forester SH 2.0D / Legacy BL 2.0D** (same EE20): identical procedure. Housing may be in a slightly different position in the engine bay.
- Always use a quality branded filter — cheap filters on diesel common rail systems are a false economy.
- If you notice black or grey sludge in the housing, get the fuel tank checked.

---

## Disposal

Fuel filter cartridges contain diesel — dispose as **hazardous waste**. Most auto parts stores and recycling centres accept them.
